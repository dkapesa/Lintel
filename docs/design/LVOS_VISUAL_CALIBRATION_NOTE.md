# Lintel Visual Calibration Note

**Status:** Approved subordinate guidance  
**Applies to:** LVOS-5, LVOS-6, LVOS-7 and V8A–V8C  
**Authority:** Subordinate to [LVOS v1.0](./LVOS_V1.md)  
**Purpose:** Calibrate the visual execution of the approved LVOS architecture  
**Change policy:** May clarify execution, but may not amend the normative identity, page archetypes, shell, semantic system or product principles defined by LVOS v1.0

> This note does not introduce a new visual direction. It defines the level of refinement, spatial organisation and product presentation expected when implementing the existing direction.

## 1. Fixed visual outcome

Lintel should feel like a complete engineering workstation rather than a collection of individually designed product routes.

The final product combines:

- the operational clarity of a professional monitoring or developer tool;
- the density and spatial discipline of an IDE;
- the evidentiary order of a technical dossier;
- the restraint and confidence of a mature enterprise engineering product;
- the product-led presentation quality of a premium developer-tools website.

The application must remain recognisably Lintel:

- evidence-led;
- requirement-led;
- neutral in structure;
- semantically coloured;
- compact but readable;
- quiet until interpretation changes;
- explicit about human authority.

The goal is not to make Lintel look visually dramatic. The goal is to make complex verification work feel organised, inspectable and under control.

## 2. Reference synthesis

The visual references used during calibration contribute different lessons. No single reference is the design to copy, and this note does not authorise literal imitation.

### Giga — public confidence and scale

Apply:

- large, confident product presentation;
- clear hierarchy between narrative and product evidence;
- immersive product scenes rather than small screenshots;
- deliberate use of negative space;
- strong visual pacing across the website;
- a sense that the product is already a serious working environment.

Do not apply:

- unrelated decorative imagery;
- visual spectacle that obscures product proof;
- branding treatments that conflict with Lintel’s technical-ledger identity.

### Waypoint — operational organisation

Apply:

- a clearly bounded working surface;
- stable navigation;
- compact command context;
- aligned records;
- visible selected-object state;
- a primary work area supported by a focused inspector;
- strong distinction between global navigation, local context and active work.

This is the strongest structural calibration for Lintel’s operational surfaces.

### Loopr — administrative discipline

Apply:

- grouped settings and administrative records;
- restrained bordered planes;
- quiet form and control hierarchy;
- clear control alignment;
- consistent row rhythm;
- low visual noise;
- strong scanability in dense configuration surfaces.

This informs administrative surfaces but does not turn active verification work into a settings interface.

### Cursor — split-pane inspection and product demonstration

Apply:

- multiple panes only when they preserve context while inspecting evidence;
- persistent visibility of the selected object;
- compact tabs for real sibling views;
- in-place switching between evidence, changes, decisions and history;
- product scenes that feel interactive and operational;
- smooth continuity between navigation, working surface and detail inspection.

Cursor’s value is its spatial model, not its exact colours, components or branding.

## 3. Definitive application calibration

### 3.1 One connected workstation

Authenticated routes should feel like rooms inside the same workstation.

The shell provides permanent orientation:

1. global product area;
2. contextual destination;
3. active object or route;
4. actions relevant to the current object.

The working surface should begin immediately after the command bar. Avoid large decorative page openings, oversized explanatory heroes or excessive empty canvas.

### 3.2 Three levels of spatial importance

The interface should normally express three spatial levels:

#### Level 1 — Working surface

The principal queue, dossier, administrative document or relationship view.

It receives the largest area and clearest alignment.

#### Level 2 — Inspector or decision plane

A selected case, evidence detail, contract clause, verdict or contextual configuration.

It is visually distinct but remains subordinate to the primary work.

#### Level 3 — Overlays

Drawers, sheets, command palette, menus and focused decision controls.

These temporarily sit above the working surface and use the approved overlay shadow.

Ordinary content must not simulate level-three elevation.

### 3.3 Connected planes rather than card collections

Use:

- shared exterior boundaries;
- interior hairlines;
- aligned columns;
- selected rows;
- persistent headers;
- section rules;
- restrained raised planes.

Avoid:

- one rounded card per idea;
- floating metric tiles;
- large empty card interiors;
- repeated rounded containers inside rounded containers;
- decorative shadows;
- panels whose only purpose is visual variety.

A section may be bordered when it is genuinely one operating region. Its internal records should normally be separated by rules rather than additional cards.

### 3.4 Tabs and panes

Tabs are approved when they switch genuine sibling views of one object or one work collection.

Good uses include:

- Inbox / Assigned / Awaiting evidence / Ready / Reviewed;
- Diff / Evidence / Contract / Decision history;
- Members / Repositories / Ownership / Activity;
- Current state / Workflow / Security boundaries.

Tabs must not conceal an incoherent page architecture.

Split panes are approved when engineers need to retain context while examining:

- a selected verification case;
- evidence attached to a finding;
- a Merge Contract clause;
- a changed file;
- readiness movement;
- a human decision record.

The default should remain one clear primary surface and no more than one persistent inspector.

## 4. Workspace calibration

The Risk Inbox is the operational home.

It should resemble a professional engineering work queue, not an analytics dashboard.

The hierarchy is:

1. route identity and current workspace;
2. compact operational summaries;
3. sibling queue views;
4. aligned verification records;
5. selected-case inspector;
6. actions associated with the selected case.

Queue records should feel dense, stable and column-led.

The selected row should be obvious through:

- a restrained plane shift;
- a left selection rule;
- preserved typography;
- no unnecessary font-weight increase.

The inspector should present:

1. pull-request identity;
2. verification trace;
3. recommendation and because-clause;
4. open requirements;
5. missing proof;
6. required next action;
7. ownership;
8. human-decision state;
9. actions.

No section inside the inspector should compete with the selected case title or decision state.

## 5. Case File calibration

The Case File is the application’s flagship working surface and the strongest proof of the product.

It should feel closer to a technical review dossier or an IDE inspection environment than to a long SaaS report.

### Required character

- compact;
- deeply inspectable;
- document-led;
- evidence attached directly to claims;
- clear relationship between records;
- stable reading order;
- persistent recommendation and human-decision context;
- restrained use of containers.

### Desktop composition

The approved composition remains:

- section outline;
- verification document;
- verdict and decision rail.

The document is the main surface.

The verdict rail should feel attached to the dossier, not like an unrelated dashboard card.

### Evidence presentation

Findings, evidence, assumptions, missing proof and contract clauses should use one related ledger grammar.

The user should not need to mentally connect separate cards.

Relationships should be visible through:

- identifiers;
- alignment;
- indentation;
- rule attachment;
- shared record order;
- direct links or disclosures.

### Long-document inspection

Where useful, future refinements may allow bounded sibling views such as:

- Case File;
- changed files;
- evidence;
- Merge Contract;
- decision history.

These must remain views of the same selected verification case and must not become separate product destinations.

### Human decision

The recorded engineer decision remains the final and heaviest record.

Visual emphasis should come from:

- placement;
- surrounding space;
- a distinct but restrained raised plane;
- clear outcome typography;
- actor, commit, timestamp and reason;
- the filled decision diamond.

Do not create celebratory, alarming or autonomous-decision styling.

## 6. Administrative and operations calibration

Administrative surfaces should be quiet, highly organised and secondary to active verification work.

Use:

- clear page title and one-sentence context;
- genuine section tabs;
- summary strips where useful;
- bordered groups;
- aligned settings rows;
- tables or record lists;
- descriptions in secondary text;
- controls aligned consistently.

Review Operations may use denser relationship views because it helps engineers understand:

- recurring blockers;
- repository activity;
- decision history;
- readiness movement.

It must still avoid generic charts or metrics that do not support an engineering action.

## 7. Website calibration

The website should make the real Lintel application its strongest visual argument.

It should not merely describe Lintel and then display isolated screenshots.

The visitor should experience one persistent verification case unfolding through the page.

### Visual character

- dark-first;
- premium but technically restrained;
- large application environments;
- asymmetric editorial compositions;
- meaningful scale changes;
- strong spacing rhythm;
- limited use of serif;
- truthful product states;
- no decorative AI visual language.

### Product presentation

Product exhibits should:

- reveal enough surrounding interface to feel operational;
- preserve meaningful text and evidence;
- use real application materials and semantics;
- show selection, inspection and decision context;
- avoid tiny unreadable full-screen captures;
- avoid fake browser chrome;
- avoid mockups that cannot exist in the real application.

The best public product scenes should resemble a live Lintel workstation:

- selected queue item with inspector;
- finding linked to evidence and requirement;
- Merge Contract clause inspected in place;
- readiness movement between runs;
- human decision recorded against the recommendation.

### Scale

The website may crop and enlarge the product for storytelling, but it must not hide the proof that makes the scene meaningful.

A product scene should normally have one obvious subject rather than showing every feature simultaneously.

## 8. Motion calibration

Motion should increase comprehension of the existing spatial model.

It may demonstrate:

- selecting a case and opening its inspector;
- switching a genuine sibling view;
- attaching evidence to a finding;
- expanding a contract clause;
- a requirement moving from open to satisfied;
- readiness changing after a new run;
- a human decision being recorded.

Motion must preserve:

- stable layout;
- readable end states;
- immediate access to actions;
- reduced-motion parity;
- no ambient activity.

The public website may use longer narrative transitions than the application, but both must communicate the same verification state.

## 9. Quality threshold for LVOS-5 through LVOS-7

A surface is not complete merely because it follows the correct archetype.

It must also demonstrate:

- immediate orientation;
- confident proportional balance;
- deliberate information density;
- readable line lengths;
- clear selected-object state;
- restrained containment;
- consistent alignment;
- no accidental dead space;
- coherent dark and light materials;
- deliberate desktop, intermediate and mobile transformation;
- visual continuity with the product scenes used publicly.

The final result should look credible when shown without explanation to an experienced engineer.

## 10. Explicit boundaries

This calibration note does not permit:

- a new shell;
- a sixth page archetype;
- a new colour system;
- new font families;
- imitation of a reference product;
- generic KPI dashboards;
- decorative charts;
- glass, gradients or glow;
- more panels merely to make a route look advanced;
- motion before V8;
- product mockups that differ from the real product;
- new product functionality inside visual-convergence milestones.

## 11. Implementation instruction

Every LVOS-5, LVOS-6, LVOS-7 and V8 prompt must state:

> Apply LVOS v1.0 and the subordinate LVOS Visual Calibration Note. The calibration note clarifies refinement and spatial quality but does not amend the normative architecture. Do not introduce a new visual direction, page archetype or visual primitive.

## 12. Final calibration statement

> Lintel should look and feel like a calm professional workstation in which engineers can move from a change, through evidence and explicit requirements, to a recorded human decision without losing context.
>
> Its visual quality should come from spatial organisation, typography, alignment, connected records and truthful product behaviour—not decorative styling.
>
> The real application is the centre of the identity and the website’s most important visual asset.
