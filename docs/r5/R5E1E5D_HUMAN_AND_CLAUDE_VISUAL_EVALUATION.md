## 1. Purpose

Phase 7.1D closes the visual-selection gate opened by the Phase 7.1C private surface comparison laboratory. It separates three different decisions:

1. Phase 7.1C laboratory implementation acceptance — whether the four live comparisons faithfully preserved the accepted product and created valid evidence;
2. Phase 7.1D visual winner selection — which surface direction best serves the complete live composition;
3. Phase 7.1E production propagation — a later bounded implementation milestone that has not begun here.

This phase is documentation and milestone closeout only. Cowork supplied an independent evaluation. Cowork and Claude had no product-decision authority; the human owner made the final decision.

## 2. Evidence reviewed

The evaluation used the governing Phase 7.1A contract, the accepted Phase 7.1B candidate-creation record, the Phase 7.1C laboratory record, the live four-route implementation, the Phase 7.1C package's genuine desktop/tablet/mobile captures and validation records, the supplied live-comparison recording and Cursor reference used by Cowork, the independent Cowork evaluation and scorecard, and commit `fd5d7a3`.

The four live variants were Neutral 26, extended neutral, B2 diagnostic and C2 diagnostic. No rejected candidate source was reopened. No recording was reanalysed or regenerated for this closeout, no candidate was regenerated, and no visual evidence was fabricated.

## 3. Independent Cowork method

Cowork independently reviewed the supplied recording at intervals across all four routes, the desktop/tablet/mobile screenshots, the Cursor reference, the Phase 7.1A–C records, product-truth evidence, interaction and responsive findings, accessibility/resilience evidence, resource inventories and candidate governance. It scored every variant on the same 15 product-oriented criteria using a 1–5 scale in which 5 is always the best product outcome, including where the criterion measures lowest risk or cost.

The criteria were product hierarchy, visual calmness, engineering credibility, interface dominance, interaction pacing, selected-state clarity, relationship to the white editorial canvas, compositional confidence, product-specific identity, generic AI/SaaS risk, responsive durability, mobile degradation, asset and maintenance cost, accessibility resilience, and similarity to Cursor's operating quality without imitation.

The evaluation was independent in the decision-support sense: Cowork supplied its own ranking and reasons instead of copying the preliminary human disposition. It was advisory only.

## 4. Human evaluation method

The human owner evaluated the full live composition rather than choosing by colour preference. The decision considered the complete Hero presentation, interaction and motion in context, spacing around the product object, interface dominance, responsive behaviour, mobile degradation, relationship to the white editorial page, Lintel product identity, maintenance burden and the distinction between Cursor's compositional lesson and Cursor's literal imagery.

The human owner then compared that judgment with Cowork's independent scorecard. The two evaluations converged on extended neutral. That convergence supported the decision but did not transfer authority to Cowork or Claude.

## 6. Neutral 26 assessment

Neutral 26 remains a clean, product-first and technically safe baseline. It has no asset, crop, provenance or responsive cost, and it preserves full interface dominance. Its weakness is compositional rather than functional: the accepted narrow inset reads more like a close application capture than a deliberately staged Hero. It remains a historical control only, not the selected direction.

## 7. Extended neutral assessment

Extended neutral retains the accepted `#fafaf9` paint and adds only generous space around the opaque product frame. It makes the frame read as deliberately staged, improves the relationship between the product and the white editorial canvas, and gives the live selected states and choreography more compositional room without adding a competing visual message.

It wins with zero surface asset, zero image request, no crop dependence and no new governance burden. Its current laboratory band values are evidence-backed starting values, not frozen production dimensions. The selection is of the direction — extended neutral — not a final pixel matrix.

## 8. B2 assessment

B2 adds a cool-slate atmospheric wash without adding product meaning. The visible desktop distinction is a colour cast rather than a clearer staging idea; live interaction does not make it more useful; and its wide master degrades toward a flat, structureless field on mobile. It carries the highest generic AI/SaaS risk of the four and has no compensating improvement over extended neutral.

B2 is formally retired and retained only as `EXPERIMENTAL — PRIVATE — NON-SHIPPABLE` diagnostic evidence. Amendment A2 remains unapproved. B2 receives no production path, derivative or companion.

## 9. C2 assessment

C2 is less visually discordant than B2 and its load-bearing-planes idea is nominally closer to Lintel's engineering identity. The live surface nevertheless reads as a soft warm gradient rather than a legible structural metaphor, retains the documented annulus asymmetry and loses its intended structure under narrow mobile crop. It does not materially improve the complete composition over extended neutral.

C2 is formally retired and retained only as conditional diagnostic evidence. Its prior eligibility under Amendment A1 does not amount to selection or authorisation.

## 10. Live-motion findings

All four routes preserved identical H1–H3 automatic/manual choreography, tab semantics, focus behaviour, canonical selected-state values, product-frame geometry, state persistence and reset behaviour. The outer surface remained static. Live motion supplied no B2- or C2-specific behaviour, information or pacing benefit and did not reverse the Gate 1 conclusion.

The winner is therefore decided by the full staged composition around the same accepted live product, not by different interaction quality.

## 11. Cursor-reference interpretation

The production lesson taken from Cursor is generous product staging, spacing and product scale. Cursor demonstrates that deliberate space around a product object can make the object feel composed rather than merely captured. Its literal photographic or painterly imagery is not the transferable requirement.

Extended neutral takes the staging lesson while preserving Lintel's identity, white editorial canvas and product dominance. Adopting B2 or C2 would add imagery without reproducing the reference's actual compositional logic and would risk imitation or generic atmosphere without product justification.

## 12. Responsive and mobile findings

The neutral routes remain compositionally durable across the tested desktop, tablet and mobile matrix and collapse safely to the accepted S1 plate below 360px. B2 and C2 remain technically stable but compositionally degrade under the identical centered `cover` rule: the wide 3000×1600 masters become broad, largely structureless colour fields at narrow widths.

No tall or mobile derivative is authorised. Creating one would extend a rejected image family rather than solve a production requirement. The current laboratory dimensions remain provisional and may receive only bounded responsive tuning in Phase 7.1E.

## 13. Accessibility and resilience implications

Meaningful content remains on the opaque white product surface in every variant. The diagnostic images are CSS decoration, absent from the accessibility tree, and contribute no meaning. The laboratory adds no animation. The accepted component retains its server-rendered content, reduced-motion branch, no-JavaScript fallback and interaction semantics; route CSS neutralises the diagnostic backgrounds under forced colours.

Genuine reduced-motion, no-JavaScript and forced-colours screenshots do not exist for this private package, and this document does not claim they do. Their absence is non-blocking for Phase 7.1C/7.1D because the route family is private and unlinked, no candidate image proceeds, the accepted interaction system already passed these states, the lab adds no animation, the candidate surfaces are decorative CSS, and Codex validated server-rendered content and forced-colours neutralisation.

That classification does not waive production validation. Phase 7.1E cannot be accepted until the propagated implementation passes genuine reduced-motion, no-JavaScript, forced-colours, keyboard, mobile and 200% zoom validation.

## 14. Asset and maintenance implications

Extended neutral adds no asset request, format choice, encode, derivative, provenance chain, crop family or asset-loading dependency. B2 and C2 each add an SVG request in the private laboratory and would require ongoing crop, accessibility, provenance and governance maintenance without producing superior value.

Neither image treatment proceeds. No AVIF/WebP encoding, public asset, tall/mobile family or Readiness companion is authorised. Readiness remains neutral.

## 15. Product-identity implications

Lintel's distinctive identity comes from its traceable product evidence, provenance, risk, requirements and human decision boundary. A decorative wash does not strengthen those signals. Extended neutral protects them by increasing compositional confidence without introducing a second message.

The selected direction is therefore product-led and Lintel-specific through staging discipline, not through a generic abstract background.
